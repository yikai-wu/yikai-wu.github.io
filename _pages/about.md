My name is Yikai Wu <a href="https://www.name-coach.com/yikai-wu" target="_blank" title="NameCoach"><i class="fa-solid fa-volume-high"></i></a> (pronounced ee-keye uu; Chinese: 吴溢恺). I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Princeton University, advised by Prof. <a href="https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~arora/" target="_blank">Sanjeev Arora</a>.

**Research interests:** machine learning and algorithms, particularly large language model (LLM) reasoning, interpretability, continual learning, LLM agents for program optimization, and AI for combinatorial optimization.

**Research brief:** My current work studies how language models acquire, represent, and combine problem-solving skills, and how this understanding can guide better training and evaluation. My research includes:

- **Mathematical reasoning and training (ongoing).** I am developing synthetic math data with controlled combinations of skills and investigating how targeted supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning affect reasoning capabilities.
- **Skill representations and continual learning (ongoing).** I extract interpretable skill representations from model gradients and use them to guide fine-tuning, with the goal of learning new skills while preserving existing capabilities.
- **LLM agents for program optimization.** I contributed to **AlgoTune**, a benchmark for evaluating whether language-model agents can speed up numerical programs while preserving correctness.
- **AI for combinatorial optimization.** I benchmarked AI methods for Maximum Independent Set against strong classical solvers and analyzed why some methods resemble greedy heuristics.

Before Princeton, I was a Ph.D. student at Yale University (2021-2022), where I worked with Prof. <a href="https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/vishnoi/Home.html" target="_blank">Nisheeth Vishnoi</a> on differentially private matrix optimization. I received my B.S. **summa cum laude** from <a href="https://www.duke.edu" target="_blank">Duke University</a> in 2021, double majoring in Computer Science and Mathematics with a minor in Physics. At Duke, I worked with Prof. <a href="https://users.cs.duke.edu/~rongge/" target="_blank">Rong Ge</a> on neural-network Hessians and Prof. <a href="https://users.cs.duke.edu/~ashwin/" target="_blank">Ashwin Machanavajjhala</a> on differential privacy.
